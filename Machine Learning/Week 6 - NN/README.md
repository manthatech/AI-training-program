## The arc

The deck and notebooks follow one thread: *a gradient signal, multiplied layer after layer, either dies or explodes.* Each technique is an attack on a specific term of the chain $\prod D^{(k)}(W^{(k)})^\top$ — ReLU protects $D$, He init protects $W$, normalization keeps $z$ where $D$ is healthy, residuals sidestep the product, clipping tames the exploding half.

## Two places the measurement overruled the tidy story

Worth flagging, because both are honest results rather than the expected ones:

1. **MC dropout's spread doesn't predict errors here.** Mean MC std is 0.1012 on correct predictions and 0.1017 on wrong ones — no separation. The predictive mean's distance from 0.5 does separate (0.365 vs 0.194). MC dropout measures *epistemic* uncertainty; these errors are *aleatoric* (the two moons genuinely overlap at noise=0.30), so every sub-network confidently agrees on the wrong label.

2. **Leaky ReLU does not rescue a diverging run.** At the learning rates where ReLU units die en masse, the leaky net diverges too. What leaky actually buys is narrower and provable: $f' = \alpha > 0$ on the negative side, so a dead unit keeps receiving signal. The notebook shows exactly that, rather than a rescue that doesn't happen.

## Suggested teaching order

Deck section → notebook, chapter by chapter. The derivation panels in the deck (the ¼ bound, the δ recursion, He's factor of ½, bias correction, $\prod(I+J_k)$) each have a matching cell in the notebook that runs the arithmetic — pause on the slide, then run the cell.

The fastest way to own this material is to break a figure and work out why it broke: change the init gain in NB2, the learning rate in NB1, or `beta2` in NB3, and predict the plot before you run it.
